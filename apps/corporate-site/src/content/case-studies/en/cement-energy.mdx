## Context

Calder Cement Works (fictional) faced rising energy costs and a board request for an emissions plan. The plant had monthly utility bills and no line-level metering, so every discussion about savings ended in the same argument about the kiln.

The team assumed the only meaningful lever was a major kiln upgrade. The data had not been collected to prove or disprove it.

## Approach

We ran a six-week metering campaign across the kiln, raw mill, cement mill, and compressed-air system, then built a load profile by shift.

- 17% of compressed-air load occurred during periods with no production demand.
- Cooler exhaust held enough recoverable heat to preheat raw-mill feed.
- Two large motors idled for more than 40% of scheduled hours.

The program sequenced no-cost operational fixes first, then a heat-recovery project with a measured baseline.

## Results

- Energy per tonne fell 11% across the metered areas.
- The site avoided roughly 9,400 tonnes of CO2e per year (fictional).
- The combined program payback was 2.6 years, with the operational fixes paying back in under four months.

## What changed

The plant now reviews a shift-level energy dashboard in the daily production meeting. The heat-recovery project was approved on its own numbers, not on a vendor estimate.
